# F4 M4.7Y: keep adjustment-transfer dialogs focused

The actual Sync adjustments and Paste selected settings dialogs share one modal. With focus on their Apply, None, Local masks or Cancel controls, application-wide G and D were able to switch the workspace behind the dialog. The fix disables those workspace shortcuts while the transfer dialog is visible and restores them after dismissal.

An actual private app using the pre-fix Root QML reproduced all 16 combinations across the two operations and four controls; its 16th-case validator returned 1. The candidate's private app passed the previous 15 guide cases plus the new transfer case on offscreen 1×/2× in 6,051/6,752 ms and Cocoa 1×/2× in 6,166/6,939 ms. The controlled tests click both operations and Cancel, retain the photo, target, history, selected groups and selection, and verify that both shortcuts resume after each close. Original results, an incomplete first probe and its corrected reproduction, and gate controls are sealed at `evidence/F4-transfer-modal-shortcuts-private-v1/receipt.json`. The compiled object stamp was the preceding pre-commit `008ffae`.

Supplementary private testing used two owned 512×320 images. Controlled pen input drew one source stroke before Copy and another afterward. The actual Sync, None, Local masks, Apply, Undo, Redo, and Paste controls were used: Sync transferred both current strokes, while Paste transferred the one-stroke copy snapshot. Source history and target exposure, saturation, rating and keywords were retained. Offscreen and Cocoa each passed 36 checks in 2,522/2,642 ms. Five screenshots per mode were independently decoded to verify the visible target preview, including undo/redo; the actual reports, ten screenshots, code and 82 rejected synthetic auditor controls are sealed at `evidence/F4-transfer-mask-flow-private-v1/receipt.json`.

These small-image experiments used controlled Qt QPA input. They do not establish physical keyboard/tablet behavior, native-system clipboard behavior, deployed packages, or completion of F0–F6. The Sony target experiment below is a separate private result.
